## 2. Founders and leadership

**Daryn Hillhouse — Co-Founder, CEO**
- Served in the British Army in Operations & Logistics, with tours in Bosnia, Kosovo and Afghanistan; afterwards managed defence contracts and bids worth more than $1B.
- Founded the Put Foot Rally, described by the company as the largest road rally in Africa, plus other adventure-travel brands; one of those brands outfitted vehicles the company calls "essentially early prototypes of Remote Vans."
- Deep overlanding experience in Southern Africa; has worked remotely around the world.
- Appointed CEO of Remote Vans (company announcement; he was previously co-founder without the CEO title being publicized).
- Sources: https://remotevans.com/remote-vans-founders-and-team/ ; https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/remote-vans-appoints-co-founder-daryn-hillhouse-as-chief-executive-officer/ ; https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/

**Tony Alexander — Co-Founder**
- Technology background. Founded or co-founded multiple tech companies, including "one of the world's leading online travel registries."
- Served on the board of a major US startup accelerator program, which he ran as Executive Director for several years.
- Circumnavigated the globe without airplanes while running multiple businesses from the road; the remote-work half of the founding idea was his.
- Sources: https://remotevans.com/remote-vans-founders-and-team/ ; https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/

**Also on the leadership page:**
- **Andrew Cooley — President.** Fourth-generation RV family; former dealership owner and manufacturer; RV Pro Magazine "40 Under 40" honoree. Source: https://remotevans.com/remote-vans-founders-and-team/
- **Robyn Hobson — CMO.** Source: https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/

**Founding story, as the company tells it:**
- During COVID lockdowns, Tony Alexander pitched Daryn Hillhouse (in Hillhouse's first-person telling on the founders page: over a bowl of pasta, after Tony "escaped" to his apartment) the idea of converting a Mercedes Sprinter packed with technology so he could work from anywhere.
- Hillhouse was at the time unable to run his annual Put Foot Rally across sub-Saharan Africa and was restoring a 1972 Ford Capri.
- They started building in an unused brewery building in the heart of Cincinnati — "Really that first van was just Daryn and I." The first batch was ~20 vans, built by a team of "less than a handful," learning as they went.
- In 2023 the company moved to a modern facility in St. Bernard (Cincinnati) and gained RVIA certification, which opened dealer distribution.
- The Friday®, Oasis® and Aegis™ Series debuted in early 2024. The T-45 was added later (it is the entry model in the 2026 lineup).
- Sources: https://remotevans.com/remote-vans-founders-and-team/ ; https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/

## 3. Build process

- **All design and build in-house** at the 30,000 sq ft Cincinnati HQ: carpentry, metalwork, upholstery, design, engineering and assembly divisions under one roof. Sources: https://remotevans.com ; https://remotevans.com/faq/ ("All our adventure vans are proudly designed and built right here in the Queen City")
- **Production line with staged QC:** the company states every van moves through "several build, testing and rigorous quality control phases" on its production line. Source: https://remotevans.com (homepage)
- **Craft positioning, not automation:** the company's own narrative emphasizes hand-learned craftsmanship (cabinetry, disciplined electrical wiring, upholstery fit and finish). **No robotics/automation claims are published anywhere on the site** — recorded here because Mark asked; the absence is the finding. Source: https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/
- **Proprietary systems fitted as standard across the range:** MoPoWa™ Power and Water Systems (aluminum housing system with easy-access service panels in the garage), the Vanhalla™ 90° swing sleeper seat, the Cyber Shower™, and the "Pennyworth" van control system. Sources: https://remotevans.com/faq/ ; https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/
- **Materials/components named by the manufacturer:** lightweight aluminum kitchen cabinetry and MoPoWa housings; solid-surface countertops; PEX-A plumbing with brass fittings throughout; insulation stack of Kilmat (sound), Solarcore® (a foam the company says was developed for NASA) and Havelock wool; Lithionics batteries; Nomadic Cooling A/C; Rixen hydronic heating; Van Compass suspension components; BFGoodrich KO2/KO3 tires. Sources: https://remotevans.com/van/aegis-series-2026/ ; https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/ ; https://remotevans.com/faq/
- **RVIA accredited** (certification gained 2023). Source: https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/
- **Lead time:** The site's own description states the vans are "ready in under 30 days" (they are spec-built, dealer-stocked units rather than custom commissions). No more precise factory lead-time figure is published. Source: https://remotevans.com (site description) ; contrast with FAQ's note that custom conversions take 6–18 months: https://remotevans.com/faq/
- **Personalization:** limited to branding kits (exterior/interior accent colors, cabinet inlays, drawer colors). The vans are otherwise fixed-spec. Source: https://remotevans.com/faq/

## 4. Warranty (exact published terms)

Source for the full agreement: **Remote Vans Materials & Workmanship Warranty**, https://remotevans.com/warranty-agreement/ (effective June 1, 2025).

- **Conversion warranty period: ONE (1) year OR 20,000 miles, whichever comes first**, from the date of original retail delivery. This is the only warranty Remote Vans itself gives.
- **What it covers:** defects in materials and workmanship of the van and its factory-installed components, under normal use. Approved claims are repaired/replaced at no cost for parts and labor, by Remote Vans or an authorized Remote Vans service provider only; new, remanufactured or refurbished parts may be used; repairs do not extend the warranty period.
- **What it does NOT cover (notable):** normal wear items and routine maintenance; cosmetic wear; misuse/abuse/accident/modification damage; environmental damage; incidental/consequential costs (towing, lodging, loss of use, loss of revenue); and — significant — **components or equipment not installed or supplied by Remote Vans, expressly including electrical systems & appliances, heaters, refrigerators, plumbing fixtures, fans, air conditioners, light fixtures and windows**. Those items carry their own component-manufacturer warranties (copies supplied with the van).
- **Transferability:** original owner only, non-transferable (unless state/provincial law requires otherwise).
- **Owner obligations:** follow operating instructions, keep to the maintenance schedule, keep service records.
- **Marketing-vs-agreement note:** Remote Vans' boilerplate repeatedly claims it "offers one of the most generous warranties available" (e.g., https://remotevans.com/company-announcements/meet-the-team-the-story-behind-remote-vans/). The actual published agreement is 1 year / 20,000 miles, as above.
- **Battery warranty (component):** 5-year / 2,000-cycle warranty on Lithionics components. Sources: https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/ ; manufacturer window stickers (Section 5).
- **Chassis warranty (Mercedes-Benz, separate from the conversion warranty), as published by Remote Vans:** 3-year/36,000-mile New Vehicle Limited Warranty; 5-year/100,000-mile Powertrain Limited Warranty; 5-year/100,000-mile Federal Diesel Emissions warranty. Sources: https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/ ; 2026 Oasis window sticker (below).
- **Cross-check:** RV Trader dealer listings print the same trio — "Mercedes-Benz factory warranties / Remote Vans 1-yr/20k-mile warranty / Lithionics 5-yr/2,000-cycle battery warranty" **[DEALER, agrees with manufacturer]**. Source: https://www.rvtrader.com/listing/2026-Remote-Vans-AEGIS-SERIES-5038797337

### 5.5 Bathroom — all models (important classification)
There is **no fixed wet bath and no black-water tank** in any Remote Vans model. The setup is:
- **Cyber Shower™:** a stowable indoor shower system — shower box with anti-slip tray, removable curtain, mixer and quick-connect shower head/hose; sets up in under 30 seconds. ## 6. Power system — MoPoWa™ (the headline feature)

Shared architecture, manufacturer-published (https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/ ; https://remotevans.com/van/aegis-series-2026/ ; https://remotevans.com/faq/):

| Item | T-45 | Friday | Oasis | Aegis |

| Battery | 51V Lithionics NEVERDIE™, **8.4 kWh** | **16.8 kWh** | **16.8 kWh** | **16.8 kWh** |
| Inverter/charger | 3,000W with 5,500W boost | same | same | same |
| Alternator charging | 48V 100A Lithionics generator alternator, "rapid recharging at any speed" | same | same | same |

| A/C | Nomadic Cooling 48V **X2** | **X2** | **X3** | **X3** |

- Battery capacity is published **only in kWh at 51V** ("16.8 kWh 100% usable battery storage with built-in reserve"). **Amp-hours are not published by the manufacturer** — do not convert and present as a manufacturer figure.
- **A/C runtime claim:** "multiple nights (3+) of off-grid A/C comfort on a single charge," varying by variant and conditions. Source: https://remotevans.com/faq/. Aegis page: X3 is "capable of multiple nights use without battery recharge." Source: https://remotevans.com/van/aegis-series-2026/

## 8. Remote-work angle (manufacturer-published)

- **Positioning:** the entire brand is built on "Work, Play, Travel … Anywhere"; the founders' own remote-work history is the founding story (Section 2). The 2026 series page answers "What is the best Class B camper van for remote work?" with its own lineup, citing power systems, Starlink connectivity and "thoughtfully designed workspaces." Source: https://remotevans.com/2026-remote-vans-series/
- **Starlink fitment by model:** T-45 — not fitted (compatibility only); Friday — Starlink **Mini** with dashboard mount; Oasis — Starlink **Standard** dish; Aegis — Starlink **High-Performance** dish. The owner must create and pay for their own Starlink account; dealers walk buyers through activation at handover. Sources: https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/ ; Friday window sticker (remotevans.com PDF, Section 5.2); https://remotevans.com/faq/
- **Workspace (Aegis, manufacturer's "The Home Office" section):** multiple workstation setups; both front cab seats swivel; L-track on walls and overhead shelf to mount second screens; one side of the bed lowers to create a **standing desk**. Source: https://remotevans.com/van/aegis-series-2026/
- **Work surfaces range-wide:** Lagun-mounted bamboo table; drop-down bamboo table; 5× 110V outlets, 3× 12V ports, USB-C (6 on Friday/Oasis/Aegis, 2 on T-45) "within arms reach" — the power system (Section 6) is explicitly pitched at "a full day of remote working in the van." Sources: https://remotevans.com/compare-adventure-vans/ ; https://remotevans.com/van/aegis-series-2026/
- The company also publishes remote-work content for owners (e.g., "How to Work Remotely from a Van: The Complete Setup Guide" in its Rolling Nomads community). Source: https://remotevans.com/rolling-nomads/

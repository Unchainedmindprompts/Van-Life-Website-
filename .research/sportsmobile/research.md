## 1. The company

- **Legal names:** The Indiana corporation is **Sportsmobile Inc.** (business listing for 250 Court St, Huntington, IN; key contact named as James Friermood). Source: https://www.chamberofcommerce.com/united-states/indiana/huntington/rv-dealer/2000458185-sportsmobile-inc . The Texas operation is **Sportsmobile Texas, Inc.** (9805 Gray Blvd, Austin, TX). Source: https://sportsmobile.com/locations/ . The legal entity name for the Arizona location could not be verified (Section 11).
- **Founded:** 1961, in El Paso, Texas, by **Charles Borskey** — the company's own history timeline says "Sportsmobile was founded by Charles Borskey in El Paso, Texas in 1961. Our first conversions were for the VW and Ford vans." Source: https://sportsmobile.com/ . The founding was a father-son start: Charles co-founded it with his father **Curtis Borskey**, originally under the name **Sportswagon**; Curtis left early and Charles ran the company for roughly six decades, until his 89th year, and died at age 90. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsmobile ; Charles Borskey obituary, https://www.dignitymemorial.com/es-es/obituaries/austin-tx/charles-borskey-10048824 .
- **What they build:** Custom, per-client aftermarket conversions of full-size vans into Class B motorhomes, plus standalone Penthouse pop-top installations, mobility (wheelchair-accessible) conversions, and service/modification work. The company states "more than 10,000 conversions" over 60+ years. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/
- **Production locations today (all three listed by the company as "Production, Service & Sales"):**
  - **Huntington, Indiana** ("North") — 250 Court St, Huntington, IN 46750.
  - **Austin, Texas** — 9805 Gray Blvd, Austin, TX 78758. The obituary above describes Austin (founded 1984) as having become the company's principal office.
  - **Gold Canyon, Arizona** ("Southwest") — 5818 S Kings Ranch Road, Suite 1, Gold Canyon, AZ 85118.
  Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/ (locations footer); https://sportsmobile.com/locations/
- **Ownership today:** Charles Borskey is deceased (obituary, above). Jim Friermood — the plant foreman who, per Wikipedia, persuaded Borskey to keep the Huntington plant open around 1980 — was identified as a vice president of the company in a 2019 Huntington County Chamber article, saying he had worked there 43 years. Source: http://sportsmobile.com.usitestat.com (mirror of the company's news page quoting the chamber article). **The manufacturer's site does not name a current owner or president; a current owner by name could not be verified** (Section 11).
- **The Fresno chapter (closed):** In 1989 Alan Feld was licensed to build as **Sportsmobile West** (San Diego, then Fresno, CA from 2000). On **September 1, 2020, Sportsmobile West ended its licensing agreement and became the independent company Field Van** — so Fresno is no longer a Sportsmobile location, and older references to a Fresno Sportsmobile plant are historical. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsmobile

## 2. History highlights (as the company and record tell it)

- **1961:** Founded in El Paso, TX; first conversions were Volkswagen and Ford vans. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/
- **1962 onward:** After his first successful VW conversion, Borskey was contracted by Volkswagen of America to install kits in VW vans, and by Ford to install camper kits at Ford's Lorain plant. The VW contract ended in 1967 (tariffs and weak sales); Westfalia then adopted several patented features of the Sportsmobile kit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsmobile
- **1968:** First full-size top, on the '68 Ford Super Econoline, using the van's metal roof with fiberglass panels, raised by pushing up spring-loaded front then rear. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/
- **1970s:** First pop-top that raised and lowered **from inside the van** — the direct ancestor of today's Penthouse top. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/
- **1971:** 4×4 conversions available on Ford Econoline vans; for years Sportsmobile used **Pathfinder** 4×4 conversions (hubs had to be locked manually). Source: https://sportsmobile.com/
- **1976:** First Sportsmobile plant opened in **Huntington, Indiana**, selling factory-direct. The plant nearly closed in 1980 (high interest rates, second oil embargo); production continued. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsmobile
- **1984:** Second location opened in **Austin, Texas** (Sportsmobile Texas). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsmobile ; obituary above.
- **1989–2020:** Sportsmobile West licensee era (Feld family, Fresno) — ended 2020, became Field Van (Section 1). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsmobile
- **2014/2015 — the Classic reinvention:** Ford ended the full E-Series van after model year 2014. Rather than kill its flagship 4×4 camper, Sportsmobile designed its own **steel-reinforced fiberglass camper shell** to sit on the still-available **E-Series cutaway chassis**, creating the **Sportsmobile Classic**, debuted at Overland Expo 2015. The shell is slightly wider than the old van body (about 4 inches more interior floor width), keeps the integrated Penthouse top, and carries the Sportsmobile-engineered 4×4 system. Sources: https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/1507-2015-sportsmobile-ford-e-series-classic ; https://newatlas.com/sportsmobile-builds-custom-fiberglass-cabin-for-new-camper-van/37413/ ; https://camperreport.com/sportsmobile-classic/

## 3. The Classic's status today

- The full Ford E-Series van Sportsmobiles were built on ended with model year 2014; the Classic (cutaway + fiberglass shell) replaced it from 2015. Source: https://camperreport.com/sportsmobile-classic/
- **Current status could not be confirmed as orderable.** As of October 14, 2026, the Classic does **not** appear anywhere in the current lineup on sportsmobile.com — the site's model pages cover only the Sprinter, Transit, ProMaster and Chevy Express — and the old Classic 4×4 page (sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-4x4/) now redirects to the homepage. No manufacturer statement announcing the Classic's discontinuation was found either. The accurate statement is: **the Classic is absent from Sportsmobile's currently published lineup; whether it can still be special-ordered is not published.** Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/ (current model pages, Section 5); redirect observed at https://sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-4x4/
- For context, Ford still builds the E-Series cutaway chassis itself (a 2026 E-350 cutaway can be ordered, and Ford has been reported to plan E-Series production through at least 2028), so the platform's disappearance from Sportsmobile's site is not because the chassis vanished. Source: https://www.autoevolution.com/news/the-ford-e-series-platform-is-over-30-years-old-heres-what-you-get-on-a-2026-model-247652.html
- Historical Classic pricing (press, not current manufacturer figures): launched from about $120,000–$125,000 in 2015; by 2020, press put typical builds at $175,000–$225,000. Sources: https://newatlas.com/sportsmobile-builds-custom-fiberglass-cabin-for-new-camper-van/37413/ ; https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/1507-2015-sportsmobile-ford-e-series-classic ; https://camperreport.com/sportsmobile-classic/

## 5. Current platforms and plans (2026, per the manufacturer)

Sportsmobile converts four chassis families today. Model designations use body codes: **RB** = Regular Body, **EB** = Extended Body, **LB** = Long Body. Source (all model pages): https://sportsmobile.com/

- **Mercedes-Benz Sprinter** — RB: 144″ wheelbase, 19′4″ length, high roof shown (low available); EB: 170″ wheelbase, 22′9″, high roof; LB: 170″ wheelbase, 24′, high roof. Factory **AWD** and diesel are the platform's headline features ("new diesel engine, all wheel drive, 3 lengths, 2 heights"). Source: https://sportsmobile.com/models/mercedes-sprinter/
- **Ford Transit** — RB: 130″ WB, 18′4″; EB: 148″ WB, 19′8″; LB: 148″ WB, 22′2″; low, medium and high roofs depending on body. Factory AWD available; the model page promotes a **"Trail Ready" off-road package** as newly available. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/models/ford-transit/
- **Ram ProMaster** — RB: 136″ WB, 17′9″; EB: 159″ WB, 19′8″; LB: 159″ WB, 20′10″; front-wheel drive, high roof on EB/LB. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/models/dodge-promaster/
- **Chevy Express** — RB: 135″ WB, 18′8″; EB: 155″ WB, 20′4″; low roof, frame chassis; the model page is titled around "Custom & 4×4 Builds" and markets ground clearance and off-road options. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/models/chevy-express/

**Floor plans.** Sportsmobile sells from a library of numbered standard plans (plan codes combine body and layout, e.g., **EB-110S**, **RB-112S**, both shown on the pricing page) and from fully custom **DYO ("Design Your Own")** plans — a current gallery build is explicitly specced as a "DYO Plan." Older Sportsmobile copy described "50 camper van plans or will customize"; the current site emphasizes custom design with a designer over a fixed plan catalog, and a current plan-by-plan catalog count is not published. Sources: http://sportsmobile.com/pricing/pricing-examples/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/gallery/sprinter-camper-van-penthouse-sleep-6/ ; https://tex.biznet-us.com/firms/2521197/ (mirror of older Sportsmobile Texas copy)

**Roof options.**
- **Penthouse Pop Top** (the signature product): custom paint-matched fiberglass top; canvas and convertible-vinyl sidewalls; a **Dual-U-Tube elevating system, manual or powered**; optional bed platform; roof rack rated to 200 lbs. Bed sizes by chassis are published (Sprinter/Transit/Chevy 40″ wide × 78″ long; ProMaster 53″ × 78″). It can be installed **as a standalone "top only" job** on an existing van, by appointment, or as part of a full conversion (the FAQ confirms top-only installs are "a very popular option"). Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-penthouse-pop-top-a-legacy-of-freedom-space-and-innovation/ ; http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Factory roofs:** low and high roofs as supplied on the base vans (Sprinter and Transit high-roof builds are common in the current gallery). A fixed raised aftermarket top under the older "Voyager" name was **not found** on the current site (Section 11). Source: model pages above.

**4×4 / AWD today.** There is no single Sportsmobile-engineered 4×4 conversion offered across the modern lineup: on Sprinter and Transit, Sportsmobile builds on the **factories' AWD systems**, with the Transit additionally offered with the Trail Ready off-road package and Transit Trail-based builds (a 2025 Overland Expo showcase build was a Transit Trail AWD with a 2″ lift). The Chevy Express page markets 4×4/off-road builds on that frame chassis. The company's own deep-engineering 4×4 (Atlas II transfer case, Dynatrac Pro-Roc 60 front axle, Fox shocks — specified on the Classic) belonged to the Classic program in Section 3. Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/models/ford-transit/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-at-overland-expo-west-2025/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/models/chevy-express/ ; https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/1507-2015-sportsmobile-ford-e-series-classic

**Packages and specialties.**
- **Standard Equipment Package** — the foundation package every conversion interior builds on. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/options/
- **Extreme weather/sound insulating package — standard equipment** on conversions. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Mobility vans** — wheelchair-accessible builds (lifts, ceiling track systems, zero-entry showers) are a standing specialty line. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/options/
- **Penthouse-only installs** and post-purchase modifications/service at all three locations. Sources: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/

## 6. How buying works

Mark's read is **substantially correct, with one refinement** (see the inventory note below): Sportsmobile is a factory-direct, build-to-order converter. There is no third-party dealer network selling new Sportsmobiles.

- **Design process:** You work with Sportsmobile's own designers/conversion experts, who "help you create your perfect adventure machine" — choose the chassis, a standard plan or a DYO custom plan, roof, and options from a large options catalog (hundreds of options per the options page). Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/who-is-sportsmobile/ ; http://sportsmobile.com/options/
- **The van itself:** Sportsmobile **can supply the base van** and says this is "the most popular way": they order through preferred dealers who know how to spec a van for conversion; freight and PDI are included in the price, and any applicable rebates go to the customer. **If you are financing the van and conversion, the vehicle must be purchased through Sportsmobile.** Customers can also bring their own van (the standalone Penthouse business and modification work presuppose customer vans, and Sportsmobile Texas will modify existing Class B campers "within certain limitations"). Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Partial work:** Penthouse-top-only installations are offered by appointment; Sportsmobile Texas modifies existing Class B/travel vans within limits. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Deposits and lead time: NOT published.** No deposit amount and no build timeline/lead time appears on the manufacturer's pricing, FAQ or process pages checked for this report. Builds are quoted individually ("Contact us for a personalized estimate"). Source: https://sportsmobile.com/how-much-does-a-sprinter-van-cost/ ; http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Delivery:** Nationwide delivery is offered. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Trades:** None — "Sorry, we don't do trades." They do sell pre-owned Sportsmobiles **on consignment**. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Financing:** RV financing for van + conversion is offered at all locations (up to 90% of total, minimum 10% down, terms up to 20 years, per the FAQ); dealers can also offer traditional auto financing. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Inventory — the refinement to Mark's read:** Sportsmobile's own site carries a live **"New Stock"** inventory (brand-new, already-built vans at its own locations — e.g., a 2023 Sprinter AWD listed at Sportsmobile Texas and a 2025 Ford Transit 148″ AWD listed at Sportsmobile Southwest) and a **pre-owned** inventory (consignment and trade-adjacent used Sportsmobiles, e.g., a 2011 Sprinter 170 EB at $35,000 and Mark-era E-Series vans), all sold from Sportsmobile's own three lots. So: no independent dealer stock and the core business is per-client builds, but a buyer who can't wait can buy a completed new or used van directly from Sportsmobile itself. Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/vans/2023-sprinter-awd-2306/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/vans/2025-ford-transit-148-awd-350/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/vans/2011-mercedes-sprinter-2500-170-eb/ ; model pages' "New Stock / Pre-Owned" sections, e.g. https://sportsmobile.com/models/ford-transit/

## 8. Warranty (manufacturer-published terms)

- **Conversion warranty: 3 years / 36,000 miles** on the Sportsmobile conversion. Stated identically in the FAQ and on the Why Sportsmobile page ("quality craftsmanship, high-grade materials, and serviceable components are backed by a 3-year/36,000-mile warranty"). Sources: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/who-is-sportsmobile/
- **Penthouse top:** carries the same 3-year/36,000-mile warranty **plus a lifetime warranty against leaks**. Source: https://sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-penthouse-pop-top-a-legacy-of-freedom-space-and-innovation/
- **Not covered by Sportsmobile's warranty:** appliances (refrigerator, furnace, water heater, air conditioner) and the **4×4** are warranted by their respective manufacturers; the **van chassis** is warranted by the van manufacturer. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Historical discrepancy, logged:** older Sportsmobile Texas marketing (mirrored on a third-party directory) claimed "50 Months/50,000 Miles" as "the best motorhome warranty in the industry." That figure does **not** appear on the current manufacturer's site; the current published term is 3 years/36,000 miles, which governs. Source: https://tex.biznet-us.com/firms/2521197/
- Service note: Sportsmobile states conversions do not void van chassis warranties, and appliances can be serviced by most RV service facilities; all three Sportsmobile locations also service. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/

## 10. Materials and build-quality claims (manufacturer's own words, paraphrased with key figures)

- **Compliance:** Sportsmobile states it meets all chassis manufacturer guidelines and complies with **FMVSS, DOT and RVIA** regulations, and highlights "quality craftsmanship, high-grade materials, and serviceable components." Source: https://sportsmobile.com/who-is-sportsmobile/
- **Insulation:** an "extreme weather/sound insulating package is standard equipment"; the company markets all Sportsmobiles as 4-season capable. Sources: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/ ; http://sportsmobile.com/options/
- **Penthouse construction:** paint-matched **fiberglass** top; sidewalls of "highly durable convertible car-top vinyl — impregnated fabric" that is mildew resistant and non-breathing (retains heat in winter, blocks it in summer); lifetime leak warranty (Section 8). Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-penthouse-pop-top-a-legacy-of-freedom-space-and-innovation/ ; http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Cabinetry/finishes in current builds:** current showcase and inventory builds specify **bamboo cabinets/cabinetry** and **marine-grade flooring** (e.g., the 2025 Transit AWD stock unit: "Bamboo Cabinets… Marine Grade Flooring"); gallery builds name finish options such as "Moderna Wood" and "Grey Wood." Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/sportsmobile-at-overland-expo-west-2025/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/vans/2025-ford-transit-148-awd-350/ ; https://sportsmobile.com/gallery/sprinter-camper-van-jmg-seats/
- **Appliances/components:** the FAQ says Sportsmobile uses "only the best quality, name-brand appliances," one reason any RV service center can service them. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Electrical (current standard/options):** standard battery package is 200Ah AGM deep-cycle marine; lithium systems have been installed "for many years," up to **1,600Ah** systems, with 24V and 48V systems available; solar up to ~500W depending on roof; all-electric conversions have been built "for well over two decades" and are now the company's recommendation for most customers. Source: http://sportsmobile.com/faqs/
- **Classic body (historical flagship):** steel-reinforced fiberglass shell of Sportsmobile's own design on the Ford cutaway — built specifically so the product would survive the E-Series van's cancellation. Sources: https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/1507-2015-sportsmobile-ford-e-series-classic ; https://newatlas.com/sportsmobile-builds-custom-fiberglass-cabin-for-new-camper-van/37413/
- **Durability framing the company itself uses:** "more than 10,000 conversions" since 1961 and a large repeat/referral customer base (older company copy notes many owners on their second, third, fourth or fifth Sportsmobile). Sources: https://sportsmobile.com/ ; https://tex.biznet-us.com/firms/2521197/

## 12. Disagreements log

1. **Warranty term:** old Sportsmobile Texas marketing "50 months/50,000 miles" vs. current manufacturer FAQ and Why page "3 years/36,000 miles." Resolved for the current figure per policy (manufacturer's current publication governs); the old figure is recorded in Section 8.
2. **Locations:** Wikipedia (and older sources) list Fresno, CA as a Sportsmobile location; the current manufacturer site lists Gold Canyon, AZ instead. Both are true at different times: Fresno was the Sportsmobile West licensee until Sept 1, 2020 (now Field Van). Current locations are Huntington IN, Austin TX, Gold Canyon AZ per the manufacturer.
3. **Founder credit:** the company timeline credits Charles Borskey alone; the obituary and Wikipedia credit Charles **and** his father Curtis as co-founders. Both recorded in Section 1.
4. **Review averages for Huntington:** 4.3★ to 5.0★ across aggregators (different snapshot dates); recorded as a range in Section 9 rather than a single figure.
